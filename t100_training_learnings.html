T100 Training Learnings

Working notes from the Singapore T100 bike GPX, Goa 70.3 bike feeler GPX, aero-position limitation, and heat/humidity risk. The goal is to make training choices clearer for a sub-5 T100 attempt.

Last updated: 26 July 2026 Race target: T100 Singapore sub-5 Working format: 2 km swim, 80 km bike, 18 km run Raceplan Border pacing plan Run form fixes

Bottom Line

Course Read

Singapore is shorter than Goa and has similar climbing per km, but it is much more technical. The course rewards clean handling, fast aero re-entry, and controlled accelerations more than long uninterrupted steady-state riding.

Aero Read

The turns help because they create natural micro-breaks from aero. They do not remove the need for aero comfort. The race skill is repeatable aero blocks, not one heroic continuous aero hold.

Weather Read

Singapore in April should be treated as hot, humid, and sweat-rate limited until race-week forecast says otherwise. Heat management has to be trained, not improvised.

Aero Training Commentary

What The Course Helps With

  • Frequent corners and U-turns create natural chances to sit up briefly.
  • You probably do not need uninterrupted 30-40 minute aero holds.
  • The practical race demand is repeated 5-15 minute aero chunks.
  • Short breaks can help neck, shoulders, lower back, and hip angle if you re-enter aero quickly.

What The Course Does Not Solve

  • Sitting up too often will cost speed and force extra watts after corners.
  • Technical courses punish power spikes because every surge taxes the later run.
  • If aero creates lower-back debt, the run split becomes the casualty.
  • A sub-5 attempt still needs a position you can return to calmly again and again.

Progression To Build

Start with 5 x 5 min aero with 2 min upright. Build toward 4 x 10 min, then 3 x 15 min. Race-specific work should use 10-15 min aero blocks interrupted by 20-40 sec sit-up or turn simulations, then a short brick run.

Weather And Humidity Risk

Singapore Risk

  • April should be assumed hot, humid, and high sweat-rate until the race-week forecast is available.
  • Humidity reduces evaporative cooling; perceived effort can rise even when power is controlled.
  • Wet roads are plausible, which makes cornering discipline and tire confidence more important.
  • Hydration, sodium, and cooling should be practiced during long rides and bricks.

Goa Feeler Weather

  • The Goa GPX ride window was roughly 07:51-10:58 IST on 9 Nov 2025.
  • Historical conditions near the route: 24.6-31.4 C air temperature, apparent temperature up to 33.8 C.
  • Humidity dropped from about 78% to 40%; rain was 0 mm during the ride window.
  • Wind was modest at about 7-8 km/h, with gusts around 18-21 km/h.

Training implication: Goa was warm but manageable. Singapore is more likely to be humidity-limited, so the race plan should include conservative early bike effort, steady fueling, and no avoidable surges before the run.
